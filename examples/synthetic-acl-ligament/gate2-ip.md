# Gate 2 · IP screen · Synthetic ligament for ACL surgery

**Verdict — Unknown.** The category is open: no in-force claim was found that covers a synthetic ligament for ACL reconstruction as such, and the broad families from the 1980s–2000s have run out. What remains are species claims — on degradable braided scaffolds, on spacer-textile composites, on hollow-braid adjustable loops with specific fasteners — and whether any of them reads depends on three choices the input does not make: material, structure, and whether the ligament is permanent or resorbable. This is a preliminary screen of public records, not legal advice and not a freedom-to-operate opinion. **The question, and who answers it:** the founder settles elements 4, 5, and 8 below; the screen re-runs on the answer.

## Fail conditions

*Written before the search. Each resolves to cleared or triggered.*

### ✓ Is there a blocking patent?

**Fail if** an in-force claim reads on elements 1–3 (a synthetic ligament body, for ACL reconstruction, fixed to bone at both ends), has seven or more years of estimated term left in the US, and there is no evidence of a licensing path.

**Cleared on the pages opened** — no opened claim reads on elements 1–3 alone; every claim that could read requires a material, a structure, or a degradation profile the input does not state. The one family with seven or more years left (US 11,357,613 and siblings, "into 2040") requires a two-layer spacer textile with an internal porous matrix.

### ✓ Is the whole category owned?

**Fail if** an in-force US claim covers a synthetic intra-articular ligament for ACL reconstruction with no limitation on material or structure, held by a company that sells a competing synthetic ligament, with estimated expiry past 2033.

**Cleared** — no genus claim found in force. The LARS process family shows "Expired – Lifetime, expires 2026-02-20"; the liquid-crystal-polymer cord claim (US 6,599,319) shows "Expired – Lifetime"; every other broad "prosthetic ligament" family surfaced was granted before 2000 and was not opened.

## Verdict

**Unknown.** The genus is free: nothing in force was found that covers a synthetic ACL ligament fixed to bone as such, and the two fail conditions clear on the pages opened. But every live claim that could read turns on three choices the input leaves open — the material (element 4), the structure (element 5), and whether the ligament is permanent or resorbable (element 8) — and two families from strategics could not be read at all. **Routed to the founder:** settle elements 4, 5, and 8 and re-run. On the pages opened, a permanent, single-polymer braided or woven cord fixed by conventional means is absent at least one element of every in-force independent claim read here, and the re-run would move to **Clear** with the risk list below; a resorbable scaffold with distinct end and middle sections, or a spacer-textile composite, moves straight into the Biorez/CONMED families and to counsel.

### What to ask for

### Possible novelty, for counsel

Nothing recordable until elements 4–8 are specified; the opened claims cover specific materials and structures, and the space between them is where a filing would sit.

### Pointers

- **Do not disclose publicly** — no paper, poster, pitch, or website describing the design — before counsel has advised on filing. Disclosure can cost rights in most of the world.
- **Status and expiry on a patent page are estimates.** Counsel verifies on the office's register; two of the families above showed no status at all.
- **An application is not a patent, but it can become one** with broader or narrower claims than it shows today. Biorez has said more applications are pending. Watch them.
- **"Patent pending" on a competitor's product means an application exists.** Find it.
- **Family members in each jurisdiction can carry different claims.** The LARS family alone has EP, CN, CA, ES, FR, PL, and RU members.
- **Keep the element list current.** Choosing a resorbable scaffold or a spacer textile re-runs every map above.
- **A crowded field is a risk; one in-force claim on an essential element with no way around or through it is a wall.** This field is crowded with expired genus patents and live species patents. Do not read the first as the second.
